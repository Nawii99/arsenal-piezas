# Open-to-Offers Post Without Sounding Desperate

**Type:** Prompt · **Area:** Jobs & résumé · **License:** CC BY 4.0 · [Spanish original](../../../prompts/empleo/abierto-a-ofertas-sin-desesperar.md)

A prompt that writes the LinkedIn post announcing you are open to work, with a confident tone instead of a begging one, ready to paste.

## What it is and what it does

Prompt that writes the LinkedIn post in which you announce that you are open to offers, in the tone of a professional who makes decisions rather than one who begs. Input: your position, experience, what you can demonstrate, work arrangement, and availability; output: a short post ready to paste. Works with Claude, Codex, Gemini, or a local AI; check that every detail is true before publishing it.

## The prompt (copy it whole)

```text
Act as a technical recruiter who has spent ten years reading LinkedIn posts from people looking for work. You know which ones generate messages from companies and which ones only generate encouragement from acquaintances.

Write me the post in which I announce that I am open to offers. Plain English, speaking directly to the reader, short sentences.

MY DETAILS
- Position I am looking for: [EXACT POSITION, FOR EXAMPLE: LEVEL 1 IT SUPPORT TECHNICIAN]
- Where I come from: [YOUR PREVIOUS JOB AND HOW MANY YEARS]
- Why I am leaving or changing: [CONTRACT ENDED / LAYOFF / I WANT TO CHANGE INDUSTRY / I HAVE FINISHED MY TRAINING]
- What I know how to do and can demonstrate: [3 OR 4 CONCRETE THINGS WITH TOOLS]
- Proof I can show: [LINK TO GITHUB, PORTFOLIO, OR PROJECT, OR WRITE NONE]
- Area and work arrangement: [CITY] and [ON-SITE / HYBRID / REMOTE]
- Availability: [IMMEDIATE / 15 DAYS / FROM DATE]
- Type of contract or internship I accept: [PERMANENT, TEMPORARY, INTERNSHIP, DUAL VOCATIONAL APPRENTICESHIP]

TONE RULES
- No pity. Do not write "anything will do," "I'm going through a hard time," "please share," "I need an opportunity," "I just ask that someone trust me."
- No fluff either. Do not write "passionate," "ready for the next challenge," "life has taught me."
- Zero emojis except one, and only if it adds something.
- Maximum 120 words. The first three lines are the only ones visible without clicking "see more": that is where the position I am looking for and what I know how to do go.
- If I am unemployed, say it in one sentence, without drama and without justifying myself.
- Let it show that I know what I bring, not that I hope someone will rescue me.
- End with a concrete, easy-to-fulfill request: who I want to write to me and about what. Asking people to share it goes at the end, in half a line, or it is left out.

WHAT YOU RETURN
1. Three versions of the post, with these tones: sober, direct, and slightly more personal. Numbered.
2. Under each one, a line saying who that tone suits best.
3. A separate 40-word message to send privately to a specific contact, with a blank for the name.
4. A list of 5 things I need to have done before publishing (photo, headline, turn on the recruiter alert, visible contact, latest project uploaded).
5. An honest warning: which part of my message will fall apart if in the interview they ask me to prove it.

Do not invent any detail that is not above. If something important is missing, ask me before writing.
```
