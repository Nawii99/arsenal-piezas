name: auditor-cv-web
description: Reviews an existing web résumé or portfolio and delivers a prioritized improvement report, used as a sales step before the site overhaul.
---

## What it does
Analyzes a URL or a code dump of a résumé or portfolio page and returns a short report with the most important problems first, designed so the client understands why it is worth paying to fix it.

## When it is invoked
It is activated when the user pastes a URL of a web résumé or portfolio and asks for a review, an audit, or a "what's wrong with this website".

## What to ask before starting
- The site's URL or, if it is not accessible, the HTML pasted directly.
- What type of company or client the site's owner wants to target, because the same flaw weighs differently depending on the goal.
- Whether the report is for the site's owner or to present to a potential client of yours before selling them the overhaul.

## Step by step
1. First review what is visible on mobile in the first three seconds: whether the name, position, and a contact detail are visible without scrolling.
2. Check approximate load speed from the weight of images and fonts, without needing external tools if they are not available.
3. Check that the contact details work: email links with mailto, phone with tel, LinkedIn that opens properly.
4. Review the text: whether the achievements have figures or are vague phrases like "responsible for various tasks".
5. Review the meta tags for sharing on social networks (og:title, og:description, og:image) and whether there is a favicon.
6. Order the findings from greatest to least impact on whether that website gets a response from a recruiter or a client.

## What to return and in what format
A report of one page at most with three blocks: 'What urgently needs fixing' (3 points at most), 'What improves things but is not critical' (4 points at most), and 'What already works well'. Each point in one sentence, without technical jargon, because it is often read by the site's owner and not by a programmer.

## What NOT to do
Do not give the report as a list of faults without context: each point must explain why it matters for getting work or clients, not just that it is 'wrong'.
Do not copy text or images from the audited site to reuse them in another project.
Do not promise in the report a specific result (more interviews, more clients) from fixing the points listed.
