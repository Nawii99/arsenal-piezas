## What it does
Adapts the lyrics of a song to another language so that they can be sung to the same melody, instead of making a literal translation with too many or too few syllables.
It is used to dub the musical number of a video, adapt a jingle to another language, or cover your own song in another market.
It saves the back-and-forth between translator and musician: matching syllables and accents by hand takes hours per song; this reduces it to minutes as a first draft.

## What to ask before adapting
The original lyrics with their language, the target language, and if possible an audio or some indication of where the strong accents of the melody fall (which syllable of each line is lengthened or stressed).

## Step by step
1. Count the syllables of each original line and mark where the musical accent falls, which normally coincides with the stressed syllable of the word in that line.
2. Translate the general meaning of each line without obsessing over literalness; prioritize that the idea gets across and that rhyme and meter fit.
3. Adjust the new line so it has the same number of syllables (or very close) as the original, and so that the stressed syllable of the new phrase falls where the musical accent fell.
4. When an idea doesn't fit in the same way, simplify it before inserting filler syllables ('uh', 'oh', 'yeah') that stand out as patches.

## What to return
Two blocks: the original lyrics line by line and the adapted lyrics line by line, with the syllable count of each so it can be checked.
If the original song has a known author who is not the user, remember that adapting it and using it in a commercial dubbing requires permission from the rights holder, even if it is in another language.

## What NOT to do
Do not deliver a literal translation without adapting the meter: if it doesn't fit for singing, it is useless for what is requested, even if the meaning is perfect.
Do not say the adaptation is '100% singable' without anyone testing it with the real melody; deliver it as a first draft that needs a final adjustment with the singer or musician.
