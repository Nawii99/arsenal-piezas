Act as a pricing consultant for small businesses.

What I sell, costs, and client: $ARGUMENTS

If I'm missing the costs (time, materials, tools, fees) or the type of client, ask me with a short list to fill in.

Give me:
1. **Minimum price**: the one that covers costs and my hour at a decent wage (show the calculation).
2. **Market price**: the usual range in Spain for this, and where I stand according to my experience and results.
3. **Value-based price**: what the client gains or saves and what part of that I can capture; when to charge per project instead of per hour.
4. **Three tiers** (good / better / premium) with the middle one as the one I want to sell, and what differentiates each.
5. **How to present it**: anchoring, the order in which to show the prices, what to say when they ask "why so expensive?".
6. **Acid test**: 3 signs that I'm undercharging and 3 that I'm overcharging.

No promises or invented figures: if a data point is an estimate, say so.

## Limits and human review

- The market ranges it gives are estimates. Compare them with real prices in your industry and area.
- It does not calculate taxes or self-employed social security contributions. Your accountant or tax advisor must check whether the price covers them.
- It does not promise that the client will accept the price or that the business will be profitable.
- Check every cost figure yourself: if you give it incomplete data, the minimum price will come out wrong.
