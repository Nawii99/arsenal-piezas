Act as an art director specializing in brand identity for small businesses.

My business: $ARGUMENTS

If I haven't given you the name and what I do, ask me. Also ask me these 3 questions before you start: Which 3 adjectives describe how I want the brand to feel? Which 2 brands (from any industry) do I like the look of? Where will the logo be seen most (social media, packaging, website, signage)?

With my answers, give me:

1. **Brand concept**: the personality in one paragraph and 3 possible visual territories (e.g., geometric minimalist, craft with character, modern tech) with the pros and cons of each for my case.
2. **Color palette**: 5 colors with their hex codes, what each one conveys, and which one is dominant. Check that text is legible on the proposed backgrounds.
3. **Typefaces**: 2 combinations (heading + body) available for free on Google Fonts, with the reasoning behind each.
4. **Designer brief**: the commission written and ready to send (what to do, what to avoid, deliverables: primary version, reduced/icon version, negative version).
5. **AI prompts**: 3 prompts in English to generate logo proposals in Ideogram or Recraft (the best for logos with text), one for each visual territory from point 1.

Honest warning included: tell me what I should check legally before falling in love with a name or symbol (trademark registration, available domains).

## Limits and human review

- It does not guarantee that the name or symbol is available: before using them, search the Spanish Patent and Trademark Office (OEPM) and the EUIPO databases and consult an industrial property agent.
- AI-generated logos can resemble existing brands; check for similarity before registering or printing.
- Check the commercial-use terms of the AI tool you use to generate the logo.
- Trademark registration and possible conflicts should be reviewed by a lawyer or a trademark agent, not by the AI.
