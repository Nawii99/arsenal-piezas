## What it does
It helps review, app by app, which permissions are granted on the phone (camera, microphone, location, contacts, SMS, storage) and says which ones are surplus based on what that app actually does.
## Before starting, ask
- Which phone they use: Android or iPhone, and whether they know the approximate version (it need not be exact).
- Which apps concern them or whether they want a general review of all of them.
- Whether they already have the permissions screen open or need you to tell them how to get there.
## Step by step
1. Explain how to get to the list of permissions: on Android, Settings > Apps > [app] > Permissions, or Settings > Privacy > Permission manager to see it by type of permission. On iPhone, Settings > Privacy & Security > [type of permission].
2. Ask them to read aloud or paste which permissions each app of concern has enabled.
3. For each permission, assess whether it makes sense: a flashlight app does not need contacts or location; a messaging app does need the microphone if it does voice notes, but does not need constant location if you do not use it for that.
4. Point out specifically the 'location all the time' (Android) or 'always' (iPhone) permissions, which use the most battery and data without being noticed.
5. Propose switching to 'only while using the app' or 'ask every time' instead of removing the permission entirely, if the app needs it to work.
6. If you detect an app with permissions that do not match what it does (for example a flashlight app with access to SMS), recommend uninstalling it outright, not just removing the permission.
## What to return
A short list per app: which permissions it has, which are surplus, and the exact change to make (remove, set to 'while using', or uninstall the app). No technical jargon.
## What NOT to do
Do not tell them to install any third-party 'cleaner' or 'security' app to do this: the phone's own settings already do it and are free. Do not promise that removing permissions makes the phone faster; that is unrelated. Do not touch or suggest touching root or jailbreak settings: that voids the warranty and exposes the phone to more risk, not less.
