## What it does
Searches for a name in the Spanish trademark register (OEPM, the Spanish Patent and Trademark Office) and in the European Union trademark register (EUIPO), checks whether the domain is available in several extensions, and checks whether the username is taken on the main social networks. With all of that it builds a report with a traffic light (free, doubtful, taken) that you can deliver to a client as a paid service.

## What to ask before starting
- The exact name, with capitals, accents, and any variants you can think of (hyphenated, unhyphenated, plural).
- What the business does, to know the Nice class (a clothing brand is not the same as a software brand).
- Whether the client wants protection only in Spain or also in the European Union.
- The domain extensions they are interested in (.es, .com, .eu, .io...).

## Step by step
1. Search for the name and close variants in the OEPM's public database (online office, trademark search).
2. Also search in the EUIPO search tool (eSearch plus), because an EU trademark also blocks in Spain.
3. Check domain availability in the requested extensions with a whois or a domain checker.
4. Check whether the username is available on Instagram, X, and LinkedIn with a simple search.
5. Classify the result as green (nothing similar), yellow (similar trademarks exist, a professional's review is advisable), or red (an identical or very similar trademark already exists in the same class).

## What to return
A short report: name searched, OEPM result, EUIPO result, free/taken domains, free/taken social handles, final traffic light, and a one-sentence recommendation. If you are going to charge for this, deliver it as a PDF or document with your branding, not as a loose list of results.

## What NOT to do
- Don't say a name 'is free' just because it doesn't appear in your search: it is an indicative search, not an official novelty search.
- Don't present it as legal advice. If the result is yellow or red, tell the client to have an industrial property agent review it before spending money on fees.
- Don't omit that the OEPM charges official fees on top of your fee for the report.
- Don't promise that the trademark will be granted: the OEPM can refuse it even if the name seemed free.
