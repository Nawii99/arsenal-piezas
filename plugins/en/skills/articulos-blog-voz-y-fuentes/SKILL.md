# Blog articles with brand voice and verified sources
## What it does
It writes an article for a company's blog, or reviews one you already have, with three rules.
- It always sounds the same: it follows a voice sheet.
- Nothing is claimed without a source.
- Anything doubtful is flagged so that a person validates it.
It delivers a draft. It does not publish.
## Ask before starting (all at once)
- Who you are writing for: which reader, what they already know, and what problem they have.
- Topic, and what the reader should be able to do or understand by the end.
- Material you can use: documents, data, links, an expert's notes. Without material, the article will be shorter and with flagged gaps.
- Voice: two or three texts of yours that you like, or a voice sheet already made (informal or formal address, tone, words to use and avoid).
- Format (guide, tutorial, comparison, opinion), length, and final call to action.
- Who will review the technical part. If nobody, you say so and it is written in the draft.
- What people search for on this topic, in their own words. If you do not know, propose three phrases and warn that they have not been measured.
## Step by step
1. Voice sheet, eight lines at most, built from your texts. It shows it and waits for your approval.
2. Summary of the assignment in three sentences: reader, promise of the article, and what is asked at the end. Wait for your OK.
3. Outline: provisional headline, intro, and four to seven sections with one sentence each. Wait for your OK.
4. Draft with short sentences, one concrete example per section, numbered steps where there are steps, and common mistakes. No filler or empty openers such as "in today's world."
5. Missing facts: write [PENDING FACT: what is missing] in the text. They are not invented.
6. Verification table: every figure, name, version, price, date, or rule that appears in the text, with its source and a status. CONFIRMED (the source is your material), PENDING (someone must look at it), or NO SOURCE (it is removed or confirmed).
7. Publication details: title for search engines (about 60 characters), meta description (about 155), short page address (slug), and alt text for each image.
8. Questions for the expert: three to eight specific things they must validate.
## What it returns
A document in this order: voice sheet, article, publication details, verification table, and questions for the expert.
## What NOT to do
- Do not invent facts, quotes, studies, names, versions, or links. If it is not in your material, flag it.
- Do not copy paragraphs from other websites or reword them. If you rely on a source, link to it and retell it in your own words.
- Do not force in keywords. You write for the reader.
- Do not promise Google rankings. Nobody can guarantee them.
- Do not generate dozens of articles at once. Google treats mass-produced, low-value content as spam. One article at a time, with review.
- Do not change voice halfway through the text.
- Do not publish. If there is a connection to your content management system, only save as a draft.
- Do not use images without a license.
- In health, legal, finance, or security, do not give personal advice. Informational text with a notice that it does not replace a professional.
## What a person reviews before using the result
- A person who knows the topic reviews the entire verification table and each technical section. In health, legal, finance, or security, a professional in the field.
- Another person (or the same one) checks voice, links, and images.
- Decide whether you disclose that you used AI. The rules are changing and some platforms require it.
- If the text talks about real people, quotes someone, or uses customer photos, you need their permission.
