# Record the video resume with your phone

This skill is for recording a 60-90 second video, talking to the camera, with an Android phone. Nothing needs to be bought. If the user doesn't have a script yet, send them first to `/guion-videocv`.

## What to ask before starting
At most three questions:
1. Where the video will be viewed: email or job portal (landscape), LinkedIn (landscape or vertical, both work), TikTok or Instagram (vertical). This decides how the phone is held.
2. What they have on hand: a window with daylight, a quiet spot, something to rest the phone on at eye level (stack of books, tripod, stand), wired earphones with a microphone.
3. Whether they will record alone or there is someone who can watch the screen.

## Step by step

### 1. Prepare the phone (5 minutes)
- Airplane mode on. A call in the middle of a take ruins it and, on some phones, cuts the recording.
- Check free space: one minute at 1080p takes up quite a bit. With less than 2 GB free, delete things before starting.
- Camera settings: 1080p at 30 fps. 4K is not needed and the file weighs four times as much.
- Clean the lens with your shirt. It is what gets forgotten most and what shows most.
- If recording alone: the rear camera looks better, but you can't see yourself. Do a 10-second test take, watch it, fix things, and then record. If that is overwhelming, use the front camera and that's it: it is good enough for a video resume.
- Paste the script into a free teleprompter app (search for "teleprompter" in the Play Store) or into notes with large text, and place it as close to the lens as possible. If the text is far from the camera, it shows that you are reading.

### 2. Light
- The light goes in front of the face, never behind. Sit facing the window, with the phone between the window and the person.
- Never with a window or lamp behind: the face comes out dark and the camera can't fix it.
- If there is no daylight, two household lamps in front, one on each side, slightly above eye level. Turn off the ceiling light if it casts shadows under the eyes.
- Avoid lights of different colors at the same time (a yellow one and a white one): skin looks odd.

### 3. Sound
- It matters more than the image. A video with mediocre image and good sound gets watched all the way through; the reverse does not.
- A small room with curtains, a bed, or a sofa: it absorbs echo. Avoid kitchens, bathrooms, and empty hallways.
- Close windows, turn off the fridge if it hums, and let people at home know.
- Phone less than one meter from the mouth. If it is farther, use wired earphones with a microphone, with the cable hidden under your shirt. Wireless ones usually sound worse for this.
- 10-second test take, listen with headphones. If there is echo or noise, change location before continuing.

### 4. Framing
- Phone at eye level. Not from below (double chin, ceiling) and not from above (childlike gaze).
- Head and shoulders. A bit of space above the head, not half the frame of wall.
- Look at the lens, not the screen. A trick: stick a small sticker next to the lens.
- Clean background without distractions: a plain wall, a tidy shelf, a plant. No unmade beds or hanging clothes.
- Plain clothing, without thin stripes (they look strange on camera) or brand logos.

### 5. Recording
- Record the script in blocks (hook, proof, fit, close), not in one go. Three takes per block. Then pick the best of each and join them when editing.
- Leave two seconds of silence before starting to speak and two at the end. It makes cutting easier.
- If you flub a sentence: don't stop recording. Pause, breathe, repeat the sentence from the start. It gets cut afterwards.
- Smile a little at the start and at the end. The first and last images are the ones people remember.
- Speak somewhat slower than feels natural. On camera everyone goes fast.

### 6. Review before accepting a take
Watch the take with headphones and check:
- Every word is understandable.
- The eyes look at the camera almost all the time.
- There is no background noise, cuts, or cars.
- The face is well lit and the background doesn't distract.
- The take lasts what the script said. If it lasts twice as long, you are improvising: go back to the script.

## What to return
When the user says they are going to record, give them the checklist above summarized in 10 lines so they can have it on their phone while recording. When they say they already have the takes, move them on to editing (prompt "Video resume editing plan in CapCut" or subagent `revisor-videocv` if they want an opinion first).

## What NOT to do
- Don't recommend buying a microphone, ring light, or tripod before having tried what is at home. A window, books, and earphones are almost always enough.
- Don't use the phone's zoom: it loses quality. Move the phone closer instead of zooming.
- Don't use beauty filters or portrait mode for this: they blur ears and hair and it looks artificial.
- Don't record in a public place or with recognizable people behind you without their permission.
- Don't record in landscape if it is going to TikTok or Instagram, or in vertical if it is going to an email or a job portal. Changing it afterwards means cropping and losing image.
- Don't accept a take without having listened to it with headphones. On the phone's speaker you can't hear the echo.
