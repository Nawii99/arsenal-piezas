## What it does
Calculates the estimated 1RM, working percentages per set, and week-by-week load progression, to avoid the two most common mistakes: adding weight without criteria and not noticing that you have been stalled for weeks.
## What to ask before starting
- Specific exercise (the squat 1RM is no use for calculating the bench press one).
- Weight lifted and repetitions done in the last set to failure or near failure (RPE 8-9).
- Goal: strength (sets of 1-5 reps), hypertrophy (6-12 reps), or muscular endurance (12+ reps).
- History of the last 2-3 weeks if available, to see whether there is real progression or a plateau.
## Step by step
1. Estimate the 1RM with the Epley formula: 1RM = weight x (1 + repetitions/30). It is only reliable with sets of fewer than 10 repetitions and near failure.
2. Calculate the working percentages according to the goal: strength 85-95% of 1RM, hypertrophy 65-80%, endurance 50-65%.
3. Propose the following week's progression: if the user completed all the prescribed sets and repetitions, increase by 2.5-5% for upper body or 5% for lower body. If they missed repetitions two weeks in a row, do not add weight; repeat the load or reduce volume.
4. Every 4-6 weeks of continuous progression, add a deload week, reducing volume by 40-50% even if the user feels strong: it is the mistake that most people skip and the one that prevents the most injuries.
5. If the user has gone 3 weeks or more without increasing either weight or repetitions in an exercise, warn about a plateau and ask about sleep, nutrition, and stress before touching the routine.
## What to return
The estimated 1RM, the recommended working weight for the current week, and a clear sentence indicating whether it is time to increase load, maintain, or deload.
## What NOT to do
- Do not give the 1RM as an exact, immovable figure: it is an estimate, and it varies with the day, sleep, and stress.
- Do not recommend increasing load just because it is 'time' according to a calendar, without checking that the user completed the previous set.
- Do not apply the Epley formula to sets of more than 10 repetitions: the margin of error skyrockets.
- Do not ignore joint pain reported by the user in order to 'not lose progression': that is where you stop and refer to a professional.
