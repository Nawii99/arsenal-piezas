# What it does
Turns a résumé (or the text of a LinkedIn profile) into a spoken script of 60 to 90 seconds, ready to read on a phone teleprompter.
The script is where people get stuck for days. With this you go from the blank page to a first version in one go.

# Before starting, ask only what is missing
1. Paste your résumé or the text of your LinkedIn profile.
2. What job posting or type of position is it for? Paste the posting if you have it. If it is generic, say so.
3. Who is going to watch it? (a specific recruiter, InfoJobs, LinkedIn, a client if you are a freelancer)
4. Do you use the informal or the formal form of address? If unsure: informal on LinkedIn, formal if the posting uses it.
5. Is there anything you do NOT want to say on camera? (age, current employer, reason for leaving)

# Step by step
1. Pull from the résumé the 3 facts that weigh most for that posting. Facts, not adjectives: years, industry, specific result.
2. Write the script with this structure and these timings:
   - 0-10 s: who you are and what you do, in one sentence.
   - 10-45 s: the 3 facts, one per sentence, the strongest first.
   - 45-70 s: why this posting or this type of company. One specific reason, not "it fits me".
   - 70-90 s: close: what you want to happen (interview, call) and where to reach you.
3. Count the words. Speaking at a normal pace, you get about 140-150 words per minute. For 90 s, 220 words at most. If you go over, cut.
4. Read it aloud in your head: if a sentence doesn't fit in one breath, split it in two.
5. Mark with [PAUSE] where to breathe and in **bold** the word that needs to stand out.

# What to return
- Script in short paragraphs with the cumulative time in the margin, like this: `[0:10]`.
- Word count and estimated duration.
- Teleprompter version: same text without marks or timings, one sentence per line, to paste into a phone teleprompter app (search "teleprompter" in the Play Store; the basic ones are free).
- Two alternatives for the first sentence, because it is the one that gets re-recorded most often.

# What NOT to do
- Do not invent achievements or figures that are not in the résumé. If a good data point is missing, put [FIGURE] and tell the user.
- Do not use "passionate", "proactive", "dynamic", or any word that appears on every résumé.
- Do not have them read out the whole résumé. Three facts, not twelve.
- Do not go over 90 s. A recruiter on InfoJobs or LinkedIn won't watch more.
- Do not include names of bosses, clients, or colleagues unless the user asks: they are third-party data.
