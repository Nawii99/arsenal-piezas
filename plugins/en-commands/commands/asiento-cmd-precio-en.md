---
description: Calculates a product's selling price starting from its real costs, VAT, the payment gateway fee, shipping cost, and return rate, and shows the margin that actually remains in each scenario.
argument-hint: "[product] [cost] [price you had in mind]"
---

Calculate the selling price of: $ARGUMENTS

Don't give a standalone number. Do this:

## 1. Ask for what's missing

Purchase or production cost per unit. Average shipping cost. Payment gateway
fee (usually a percentage plus a fixed part). Percentage of orders that are
returned. Applicable VAT. Packaging cost. Whether there is any monthly fixed
cost that needs to be spread across the units you expect to sell.

If I don't give you a data point, **don't make it up**: ask for it, and if I
don't know, use a reference value, stating clearly that it is a reference and
where it comes from.

## 2. Break it down backwards from the price

Starting from the selling price including VAT, subtract in this order and show
each step: VAT, gateway fee, shipping not passed on to the customer, packaging,
product cost, and the proportional share of returns.

What remains is the real margin per unit. Show it as a percentage too.

## 3. Give three scenarios

- **Price I had in mind**: what margin it really leaves.
- **Price for a target margin**: ask me which one I want and calculate it.
- **Minimum price**: below what price I lose money on every sale.

## 4. Warn about what is usually forgotten

Point out which of the costs is eating the margin unnoticed, and what happens
if returns go up a few points. If the margin is below 15%, say so clearly: it
is not a business, it is a poorly paid job.

## 5. Close

One sentence with the price you recommend and why, and the two assumptions that
would change the answer the most if they turn out to be different.

Don't make sales projections or revenue promises. Only the price and the
margin, with the numbers on the table.
