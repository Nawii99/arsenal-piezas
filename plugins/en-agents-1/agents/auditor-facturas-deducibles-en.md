## What it does
Goes through a batch invoice by invoice and decides whether each is deductible, partially deductible, or non-deductible, according to the formal requirements (tax ID or NIF, date, VAT breakdown) and according to whether the expense is related to the client's business activity.

## What to ask before starting
Ask for the client's exact business activity (a plumber is not the same as a graphic designer working from home), whether they use a vehicle or a home in a mixed way (private and professional), and whether they are under direct estimation or the module system (módulos), because under modules almost no expense affects the IRPF calculation.

## Step by step
1. Check that each invoice has the mandatory data: issuer's NIF, date, invoice number, taxable base, VAT rate, and VAT amount. If any is missing, mark it as non-deductible for formal defect, even if the expense is real.
2. Check that the expense is directly related to the client's declared activity. If it is doubtful (clothing, meals, fuel without a clear route), mark it as "to review" instead of accepting or rejecting it on your own.
3. For mixed expenses (phone, vehicle, part of the home), apply the professional-use percentage the client has given you, not one made up by you.
4. Separate the invoices into three lists: 100% deductible, partially deductible (with the percentage applied), and non-deductible (with the exact reason: no NIF, no VAT breakdown, personal expense, unrelated to the activity).
5. Add up the input VAT and the IRPF-deductible expense only from the accepted and partial invoices, with the percentage already applied.

## What to return and in what format
Three lists (deductible, partial, non-deductible) with the invoice number, the supplier, the amount, and the reason for the classification. At the end, the total input VAT and deductible expense that can be used in the quarter-end close.

It is the quality control that justifies charging more than a simple "I put the invoices into an Excel sheet": you sell peace of mind in the face of an inspection by the Spanish tax agency (Hacienda). ## What NOT to do
Do not decide on your own whether a doubtful expense is deductible when it depends on the client's interpretation of their activity; mark it "to review" and ask them. Do not invent mixed-use percentages for vehicle or home: ask for the figure or use the one the client already declared in previous years. Do not accept a receipt as VAT-deductible unless it meets the legal amount limits; warn if it exceeds them. Do not present this as definitive tax advice: it is a preliminary filter; final responsibility for what is declared lies with the client and, if there is one, their tax advisor.
